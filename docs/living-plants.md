# Living procedural plants: time, growth and reproductive cycles

Design contract, 4 November 2026. This document specifies the next botanical
system; it does **not** introduce executable commands, a save format, a solver
or runtime behavior. The user paused the Cuihua collection to establish this
foundation before producing further species. Lighting remains a separate
workstream in the `lumens` chat.

## Authoring and gameplay contract

Advancing one world clock must advance the mathematical evaluation of the
whole plant: roots, stem, branches, buds, leaves, flowers and fruit. The user
must not have to increment independent growth values for every organ. Species
profiles determine how that common time affects each process.

The canonical object is a living procedural plant, with three distinct parts:

1. **Definition:** versioned growth grammar, organ shapes, species profile,
   deterministic seed and editable parameters. It describes how organs can be
   produced, not just the fully grown graph of one exemplar.
2. **Individual state:** persistent identity, birth time, realized organ/bud
   graph, physiological progress, cohorts, health/resources and relevant
   intervention state. Cuts, harvested fruit and damaged buds belong here.
3. **Derived representations:** render meshes, deformation bindings, collision
   proxies, distant representations and caches. They can be replaced without
   losing the individual's history. Leaf/fruit detachments become separately
   identified world objects when the configured gameplay requires them.

This extends the current editable botany source plus bake. It must not silently
convert existing botanical assets to static meshes or discard their curves,
manual changes or generated materials.

## One advancing time, several derived clocks

Let `T` be monotonic world time and `B` the individual's persistent birth time.
Chronological age is `A(T) = max(0, T - B)`. Calendar phase uses shared world
time, not the plant's age. This prevents trees planted in different months from
experiencing incompatible seasons merely because they have different birthdays.

A default, environment-independent profile can derive all development directly
from age. For an environment-sensitive profile, physiological progress is:

```text
D(T) = integral from B to T of q(environment(s), state(s), profile) ds
q >= 0; default q = 1
```

Here `D` has time units; it is not a normalized slider that permanently ends at
1. Temperature, water, light, damage or vigor can affect `q` when those responses
are enabled. Different organ classes may have configured response functions
derived from the same clock. Mechanical displacement from wind is a separate
pose evaluation, not another age or growth multiplier.

The intended user-facing operation is conceptually `advance_time(delta)`: the
world advances once and plants update from it. This name is illustrative, **not
an existing MCP operation**. An editor may additionally preview an arbitrary
age/date. Preview does not rewind the gameplay clock or erase interventions.

With environment-dependent development, `D` and its last evaluated time must
be persisted or reconstructible from an authoritative history. The current
temperature multiplied by age cannot reconstruct years of varying conditions.

## Growth of existing organs and birth of new organs

Every organ has a stable ID, parent/site, birth event and local developmental
progress. For organ `i`, with birth progress `d_i` and expansion duration `tau_i`:

```text
u_i(T) = clamp((D_i(T) - d_i) / tau_i, 0, 1)
s_i(T) = u_i(T)^2 * (3 - 2*u_i(T))
```

`s_i` feeds configured functions for length, radius, width, thickness, curvature,
orientation and material state. These functions are independent: radial growth
need not follow elongation, and old trees do not have decades-old giant leaves.
Smoothstep is an initial interpolation option, not a mandated biological law.

The rest curve remains attached to the evolving parent insertion/frame. The
current mechanical pose is derived afterwards. Rest geometry and a wind-bent
pose must not be confused when measuring growth or transferring edits.

New branches must be generated by eligible buds/sites as development advances.
A bud rule specifies eligibility, maturity, spacing, branching order, direction,
length/radius distributions, environmental response and production timing.
Revealing a pre-authored adult graph remains useful for compatibility/preview;
it does not by itself satisfy the requirement to generate new organs over time.

Identity must derive from the parent/site, production event/cohort, organ role
and deterministic seed. It must not derive from the current array position,
frame number, worker order or the number of earlier random draws. Removing a
branch must not reroll the shape or production of unrelated branches.

Zero-size buds may exist semantically before they have a renderable mesh.
Topology construction must avoid degenerate triangles and invalid junctions;
renderability thresholds must not redefine the recorded birth event. Invalid
geometry cannot be repaired by silently deleting the corresponding living organ.

## Repeated leaf, flower and fruit production

A species may have multiple production schedules. Each schedule declares:

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Clock | Shared calendar, age-relative or physiological progress, explicitly selected |
| Eligibility | Minimum maturity, living support/bud, branching order and optional environmental/resource conditions |
| Period and phase | Interval between production starts and phase relative to the selected clock |
| Variation | Stable per-individual/site/cohort timing and quantity variation |
| Sites and quantity | Eligible sites and bounded candidates per site/cohort |
| Stage durations | Budding, leaf expansion/lifespan, flowering, fruit growth/ripening and attached ripe duration |
| Outcomes | Fruit-set fraction and optional pollination/resource requirements; fruit/leaf drop and withering policies |
| Recurrence | One-shot, repeated indefinitely within lifetime, finite number of cycles, or configured windows |

Period must be positive, durations finite and nonnegative, and candidate counts
bounded. A schedule may cross a calendar-year boundary or overlap another
cohort. Never implement this as only a modulo-time visibility mask.

For a periodic schedule, cohort `k` has nominal start:

```text
t_k = phase + k * period + seeded_jitter(individual, site, k)
```

Bounded jitter must have an explicit ordering/overlap policy. Eligibility and
outcomes are evaluated at defined event times and retained when history matters.
Maturity is tested when that cohort starts, not using the age at a much later
render. A missed flowering window must not be retroactively created because the
tree is old enough now.

A reproductive unit follows a configured lifecycle:

```text
eligible bud -> flower bud -> open flower -> fruit set -> growing fruit
             -> ripe attached fruit -> harvested / dropped / withered
```

Unsuccessful fruit set can terminate after flowering. Flower and fruit bodies
have distinct stable sub-organ IDs under the same reproductive unit; petals
need not survive as fruit geometry. Each fruit candidate has a stable outcome
draw rather than rerolling probability on every update. Pollination simulation
is optional; an authored automatic fruit-set policy is a valid initial mode.
Profiles may suppress visible flowers or allow fruit set without the usual
flower/pollination dependency when explicitly configured.

Leaves likewise belong to cohorts: young, expanding, mature, senescent and
detached/dead. Evergreen profiles can overlap successive cohorts rather than
replacing the entire canopy on the same date. A new annual cohort gets new
identities; it does not revive last year's harvested fruit or fallen leaves.

## Illustrative fruit-tree profile

The following values use authored game days, not measured Cuihua biology. This
is a design example, **not a native `.ignis-botany` schema**:

```json
{
  "calendar_days_per_year": 360,
  "reproduction": {
    "clock": "calendar",
    "minimum_age_years": 3,
    "period_days": 360,
    "flower_open_phase_days": 80,
    "flower_bud_days": 7,
    "flower_open_days": 14,
    "fruit_growth_days": 45,
    "ripe_attached_days": 30,
    "fruit_set_fraction": 0.7,
    "pollination": "authored_automatic",
    "on_ripe_expiry": "drop"
  }
}
```

Ignoring timing variation, an eligible cohort buds at day73, opens at day80,
sets fruit at day94, ripens at day139 and drops unharvested fruit at day169.
Another cohort starts the next year if its sites remain eligible. Harvesting
at day145 removes that particular fruit; it does not restart the schedule or
spawn a replacement during that same cohort. A tree born mid-year still uses
the shared calendar and must reach maturity before a scheduled flowering event.

## History, damage and intervention

- **Pruning/cutting:** remove or detach the affected graph connection and record
  the intervention. Existing fruit/flowers on that support follow a declared
  detach/abort policy. Nearby living buds can later produce regrowth if their
  rules allow it; a cut must not simply resurrect the removed organ ID.
- **Harvest:** retain terminal state for the fruit/cohort. Save/load, revisiting
  an unloaded region and re-evaluation at the same time must not duplicate it.
- **Fire, drought, temperature and disease:** drive configured health/resource
  and structural state. A material color change alone does not implement damage
  or death. No particular fire/thermal/vascular solver is selected here.
- **Death:** suppress future biological production. Standing dead wood can
  remain until structural failure; death, drying, detachment and falling are
  distinct events. A detached fruit/branch is transferred to world ownership
  exactly once, with a declared physical state/velocity policy.

The save needs effective state plus the intervention/tombstone information
required to preserve these outcomes, with a bounded compaction strategy. A
complete unbounded event log is not mandatory; seed and age alone are
insufficient after interventions. Dead/harvested cohorts can be compacted only
when they cannot become observable again under the declared replay/save policy.

## Evaluation, performance and persistence boundaries

Biological scheduling, mechanical simulation and rendering have different
update needs. Wind normally changes pose without rebuilding the organ graph.
Topology changes occur at production, detachment, cutting and similar events;
continuous expansion may use deformation and bounded representation refreshes.
No whole-tree JSON load or full mesh regeneration is required every frame by
this contract. The exact representation/update strategy remains to be designed.

An advance interval must process all crossed relevant events in chronological
order, including flowers that open and close between two observations. Active
cohorts may include earlier periods. Performance must not depend on whether the
player happened to render the flowering frame. Offline/large-time advances can
use analytic evaluation or scheduled events where equivalent; history-sensitive
behavior needs defined integration intervals and the same environmental input.
Arbitrary jumps must not replay billions of render/physics frames.

Limits must cover active organs, candidate buds, concurrent cohorts, event work,
detached objects and derived geometry. Profiles must be validated against these
limits. A capacity rejection must be explicit and preserve the last valid state;
silent loss of organs, harvested-state resets or partial publication is invalid.
Scheduling, residency and resource-loading strategies are future design work.

Persist canonical plant state independently of transient vertex indices and
physics-engine handles. Save/replay/multiplayer and MCP/UI parity must be designed
before enabling gameplay mutation. Existing Scene/source versions and legacy
assets remain unchanged by this document; incompatible migration is never
inferred from an illustrative JSON example.

## Current implementation versus this proposal

- [Native botany](botany-integration.md) already retains an organ/curve source
  and derived bake, stable material addresses, authoring edits and pruning.
- [Age profiles](botany-age.md), implemented in `src/botany/development.rs`,
  evaluate authoring snapshots of chronological development and candidate-organ
  expansion. They do not yet provide this open-ended runtime grammar or periodic
  reproductive scheduler.
- Native XPBD botanical dynamics are a local authoring experiment; their wind,
  growth and pruning are not yet a persistent gameplay lifecycle. Source
  publication currently requires a paused editor.
- The [birch study](genshin-birch.md) preserves adult sources/materials, but its
  three sources have no development profile. Its Python preparation grammar
  must not be advertised as native runtime organ generation.
- Flowers/fruit in the mathematical V2 lab are useful shape experiments, not an
  implemented lifecycle, harvest system or native periodic production contract.

## Required implementation and acceptance sequence

1. Canonical individual/organ/site/cohort IDs and time/state contracts, including
   serialization, migration, intervention retention and reproducible evaluation.
2. Native bud/organ growth grammar: one species grows from a small plant to an
   adult and continues producing new organs without changing surviving IDs.
3. Leaf and reproductive schedules: at least two complete flower/fruit cycles,
   maturity gating, overlap/year-boundary cases, harvest and missed-frame updates.
4. Gameplay integration: structural updates, deformation/collision ownership,
   detachments and supported persistence/replay/MCP/UI behavior.
5. Environmental/health/structural extensions for temperature, fire, drying,
   death and falling, each with explicit scope and performance measurements.
6. Adapt birch/Cuihua and subsequent species to the accepted living definition;
   measure actual full-tree visuals, generation/update cost and runtime budget.

Minimum proofs include: no resurrection after harvest/cut/save/load; no duplicate
detachments; stable unaffected organs when another branch is removed; correct
events across a skipped flowering window and concurrent cohorts; maturity checked
at event time; dead plants stop production; older trees produce new leaf/fruit
cohorts rather than endlessly enlarging old ones; atomic failure on invalid
profiles/capacity; and agreement between one advance and partitioned advances
under the same canonical event/environment timeline. Cross-device floating-point
identity or arbitrary biological accuracy is not promised by these requirements.

No implementation or biological calibration is claimed by accepting this design
document. Runtime work and artistic acceptance require their own evidence.
